01 // The problem

The X500 runs out of thrust with the releaser

  • ≈ 3.4 kg at take-off with UCLA's autonomy kit, a 4S 5 Ah pack and the 1 L dummy on its mount.
  • Thrust-to-weight ≈ 1.3 at nominal battery voltage (≈ 1.6 on a full pack). It hovers at about 80 % throttle, so little thrust is left for corrections.
  • ≈ 5–6 minutes of usable flight with a 20 % battery reserve.
  • That matches the trials: altitude dips during corrections, wobble in hover and slow flight, and close passes that needed manual corrections.
  • A stock X650 does better (≈ 1.5) and copes with the 1 L releaser in our simulation, but has no margin for the 2 L or 4 L releaser. Holybro's 4.3 kg payload rating assumes no battery and 70 % hover throttle.

X500 figures are our estimates. UCLA's weighed take-off mass and flight logs will confirm them.

Why not the Starling 2 Max?

Strengths

  • Mature visual-inertial navigation (VOXL 2 computer, dual fisheye tracking cameras)
  • Small: about 0.5 m tip to tip
  • 40–55 min of flight without payload

Limits for this mission

  • Rated payload 500 g; the 1 L releaser alone is 1,073 g (2.1×), before its mount
  • 43 Wh battery: the 60 W blower alone would drain it in about 43 min, before any flight power
  • Different computer (Qualcomm QRB5165): UCLA's Jetson models would need porting
  • Lead time about 60 business days

Useful as a navigation-only test aircraft; not suited to the combined navigation-and-payload mission.

02 // The recommendation

An X650-class quad on 6S, reusing UCLA's autonomy core

More than twice the X500's thrust, room for the releaser, and no change to UCLA's software stack.

Reuse from the X500

  • Pixhawk 6X + Jetson Orin NX baseboard (with NVMe and Wi-Fi)
  • RealSense D455 depth camera
  • GNSS/compass (outdoor backup only)
  • Telemetry radio and RC transmitter
  • UBEC 12A (Jetson power)

Replace

  • Frame and motors → X650 frame, T-Motor MN4014 KV400 motors, 45 A ESCs, 16×5.4 carbon props
  • 4S packs → 6S 10 Ah LiPo (×3)
  • Optical-flow sensor → ARK Flow (flow + downward rangefinder)

Add

  • Upward rangefinder (roof and lights)
  • LightWare SF45/B scanning lidar → PX4 collision prevention
  • 12 V blower supply, switched by the flight controller, with an RC cut-off
  • Quick-release releaser mount
  • Remote ID module (if flown outdoors between tunnels)

Navigating without GPS, with the pilot always in charge

Heading comes from visual odometry because the steel hoops disturb the compass. GNSS is optional outdoors, between tunnels. Recovery uses precision landing on a printed fiducial pad.

Pilot authority

  • RC receiver wired directly to the Pixhawk: it works even if the Jetson stops
  • One switch hands control from autonomy back to the pilot (Position, Altitude or Stabilized mode)
  • Dedicated kill switch and a physical arming switch

Automatic protection

  • Failsafes for RC loss, companion-computer loss, low battery and estimator faults: hold or land
  • Geofence on a local origin, so it works without GPS
  • Blower power on its own RC cut-off; anti-spark battery connector
  • Options: propeller guards (about 300 g), Remote ID broadcast module

06 // Next steps

What we need to finalize the quote

  1. Tunnel envelope: width, height, door size, bed spacing, and the heights of supports, irrigation and lights (the aircraft is about 1.06 m wide).
  2. Reuse or new: will the X500's Pixhawk/Jetson, D455, GNSS and radios move to the new aircraft?
  3. Software: hardware only, or also a configured flight stack (PX4 sensor fusion, failsafes, Jetson drivers)? Autonomy development stays with UCLA unless you want our support.
  4. Funding: are federal funds involved? Federal rules on foreign-made drone components could change the parts list.
  5. Releaser: mass, size, mounting points, 12 V inrush and duty cycle, control signal.
  6. Acceptance: bench-verified delivery, or a short builder hover and tune?

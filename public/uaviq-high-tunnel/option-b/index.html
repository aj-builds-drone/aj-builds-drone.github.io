    Arrows run from the first sortie to the last full one. Peak motor load is the busiest motors' thrust as a share of their maximum at that moment's battery voltage. The 3-D Gazebo flight below peaks higher (the peak motor load at the top of the page): it flies in three dimensions with the full PX4 controller, crosses the gusty gap and includes the take-off. The X500 runs on its 4S 5 Ah pack, every other aircraft on a 6S 10 Ah pack. Option A and the X650 carry the releaser on a 200–250 g mount; on Option B the pod hardware is part of the frame. The X500 is left out of the 2 L table: it cannot carry it. The side-view model sees mass and pitch only, so the battery-centred reference flies like the 1L here; its sideways imbalance shows in the Gazebo flight below.

    03 // Flight in Gazebo with PX4

    The selected frame on the two-tunnel mission

    A recording of the 3-D simulation, not an animation: Gazebo Harmonic renders every image from its physics, and the aircraft is flown by PX4 v1.17, the real autopilot firmware, in software in the loop. No GPS: it navigates on vision odometry and optical flow, releases six doses over the rows, crosses the windy gap and lands.

    06 // Printed parts

    Print list by filament

    Filament

    • ASA keeps its shape in a hot tunnel and in sun (no PLA or PETG on this aircraft).
    • PA-CF (carbon-filled nylon): stiff and heat-proof, for the parts that carry motors, arms, payload and the Jetson.
    • TPU 95A: soft foot caps.

    Printed parts

    Printing on an Anycubic Kobra S1 or Kobra X

    FilamentKobra S1 (enclosed, 320 °C) Kobra X (open bed-slinger, 300 °C)
    ASAYes: the enclosure keeps it from warping Supported, but large parts may warp or crack in open air: print them on the S1
    PA-CFYes, after fitting the hardened-steel hotend (carbon fibre wears a brass nozzle out); dry the spool first No: not on its supported list (PLA-CF and PETG-CF are), and nylon needs an enclosure
    TPU 95AYes, from the external spool holder (not through the ACE Pro)Yes
